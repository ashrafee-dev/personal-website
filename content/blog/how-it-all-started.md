You know how in some places students need expensive guidebooks to pass exams? Back in **elementary school**, most families in my **village** could not afford them. Almost everyone had a **phone**.

<div class="timeline-marker">Early 2016</div>

So I started tinkering. Without Wi-Fi, learning anything meant downloading tutorials, tools, and whatever I could find on mobile data. I played with After Effects, tried Photoshop, and explored different software.

While experimenting, an idea appeared: build a **simple Android app** with free educational content. Stories, letters, and paragraphs that could help students study. I had no clue how to code, and downloading Android Studio without Wi-Fi was a mission of its own.

{{< image src="images/old_days1.png" alt="Old CRT monitor showing a code editor with terminal" caption="Development Setup (2016)" >}}

Try walking half a mile to buy mobile data with saved money, watching a download reach 50%, and then losing it to a storage-full error. Downloads corrupted, installers failed, signals dropped. Every megabyte mattered. Android Studio took 12 hours of hoping nothing would break.

My computer had only **1GB of RAM**, so every compilation tested my patience. I restarted it more times than I can count. Eventually I upgraded the RAM and learned the hard disk was too small to handle the extra memory well. Disk thrashing became the soundtrack of my coding sessions.

<div class="timeline-marker">Mid 2017 - Early 2018</div>

I had no mentors or YouTube tutorials. When I got stuck, I left it and came back later. It was isolating, but I kept going.

After months of trial and error, sleepless nights, and a progress bar that seemed frozen in time, I finally held a working APK. I could not afford the $25 Play Store fee at first, but later I managed. In **2018**, I published it. It had only a few users, but it was real and it worked.

{{< image src="images/old_days2.jpg" alt="The finished Android app showing educational content" caption="The final product, published to the Play Store in 2018." >}}

<div class="timeline-marker">Late 2018</div>

I know what you’re thinking: dude, just make PDFs and send them to everyone. That’s what I remembered in the middle of all that. But hey, you gotta begin somewhere—and sometimes you just gotta find an excuse to use something you just learned.

Not with a goal of becoming a software engineer, but with a desire to solve a problem I saw around me.
